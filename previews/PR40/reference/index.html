OpSum.Basis — Type
Basis(ops::AbstractVector; atol = 0, rtol = 1e-12)

An ordered basis of on-site operators on one space V: the alphabet that project expresses operators in and that a symbolic operator's letters index into.

Each entry of ops is charge-neutral: a single-site operator V ← V (or a plain matrix, without symmetry), used as an element as it is, or a two-site operator V ⊗ V ← V ⊗ V, factorised into its charged single-site pieces (for S⁺ ⊗ S⁻ the pieces S⁺ and S⁻), which become elements.

Element 1 is the identity, prepended; an entry parallel to it (or to another element of the same charge) is dropped. Single-site entries come first, in the order given, then the factorised pieces. The elements of one charge must be linearly independent (checked) but need not be orthogonal, and the basis need not span all operators on V: project throws for an operator outside its span.

Internally each element is a TensorMap V ← V ⊗ Vect[I](c => 1) of one charge c; its letter is (c, n), the n-th element of charge c. Pairs are factorised canonically (_factorise_pair), so a pair of single matrix units gives those matrix units, each piece's first coefficient is 1, and the pair's scale becomes its coefficient when projected.

With complete = true the basis is extended by the matrix units it does not yet span, so that it spans every operator on V (as the stock bases do): on-site products and H' never leave it.

Imat = [1 0; 0 1]; Xmat = [0 1; 1 0]; Ymat = [0 -im; im 0]; Zmat = [1 0; 0 -1]
B = Basis([Imat, Xmat, Ymat, Zmat])          # four elements, the identity first
X = project(Xmat, B)                         # one letter: X is atomic in B

OpSum.ExpKey — Type
ExpKey{I<:Sector}

One decaying interaction as a hashable descriptor: the representative term (a fully coupled Term), the exitsite where its exit block starts (factors at sites < exitsite are the entry block, the rest the exit block; both non-empty), the decay λ, and the on-site string carried by the stretched gap, as a canonical (letter, coefficient) list (nothing = identity).

OpSum.ExpSum — Type
ExpSum{I<:Sector}

A sum of exponentially decaying interactions (ExpKey descriptors with coefficients), the geometric counterpart of Terms. Built by expterm and added to an OperatorSum: opsum(lat, h, expterm(h; decay = λ)). A sum of exponentials is a sum of channels; each distinct λ costs its own bond index.

OpSum.FiniteChain — Type
FiniteChain(bases::AbstractVector{<:Basis})
FiniteChain(B::Basis, N::Integer)

An open chain of length(bases) sites, carrying one operator Basis per site: the letters of a term on site i index into bases[i]. Sites with different spaces are combined by listing their bases, e.g. vcat(Bf, Bs, Bf); all must share one symmetry. getindex does not wrap: a term reaching outside 1:length(chain) is an error.

FiniteChain(B, N) is the uniform chain. A bare vector of bases is accepted anywhere a FiniteChain is.

OpSum.ITOGraph — Type
ITOGraph{I}

The persistent bipartite graph over an ITOTermTable, handed from one site step to the next by _at_site!. A right vertex is a suffix class; classes enter at their term's first active site and thereafter only merge. The current graph (for the bond i-1 → i about to be processed) is lefts ↔ right vertices, with per-left-vertex adjacency lists radj/wadj (right-vertex id, scalar weight); zero-weight entries are never kept.

Fields:

  • fixed suffix-class machinery: K, sufid (interned id of every contiguous column suffix, see _suffix_ids), the lowered exponentially decaying channels and lastentry, the last site at which any may still enter;
  • persistent right-vertex state: rrepr (representative term id), the monotone cursor rcur/rbond behind the O(1) signature, and rchan/rstate for geometric classes (rchan[r] == 0 an ordinary term class, > 0 an automaton state of that ExpChannel, -1 the synthetic exhausted class a channel exits onto);
  • the current graph: lefts, radj, wadj, nlinks (incoming bond dimension);
  • lazy insertion (see _promote_pending!): lazy, firstsite, pend_at, pendbysig, inserted, nremaining, and per bond rsent (sentinel right-vertex id), startleft (its left vertex) and startidx (outgoing index of the start channel), each 0 if none;
  • finish channel, the mirror image (jordanmpo.jl): rfinish (right vertex of the exhausted class at the trivial charge), finishleft (the left vertex its pass-through enters on), finishidx, each 0 if none;
  • per-site scratch, so a site step allocates nothing per bond: slot, vlocal, firstleft (right vertex → first incident left vertex, which gives its bond charge), remap, siggroups.

OpSum.ITOGraph — Method
ITOGraph(tt::ITOTermTable{I}, N; lazy = true, channels = ExpChannel{I}[]) -> ITOGraph{I}

Seed the persistent graph: intern the column suffixes, create the right vertices, and bucket the initial left vertices by the site-1 key against the single left-boundary link. Θ(M·K).

With lazy = true (the default) only terms active at site 1 get a right vertex; the rest are represented by a sentinel right vertex on the identity/start channel and inserted when reachable (_promote_pending! / _build_next_graph!), which makes the sweep cost Θ(Σ_terms span) instead of Θ(N·M). lazy = false seeds every term eagerly (used by SequentialSVD, whose dense SVD dominates anyway). jordan = true forces the finish class into every cover (_force_finish!), as mpo asks on a finite chain without decaying channels.

channels are lowered exponentially decaying interactions (_lower_channels): not terms, they enter on the start channel at every site matching their phase and are thereafter ordinary (cyclic) right vertices. They require lazy = true.

OpSum.ITOTermTable — Type
ITOTermTable{I<:Sector}

Flat, sparse-per-term storage of a canonical ITO operator on an N-vertex chain: each term's active (site, ITOKey) factors in K×M matrices (sites zero-padded, ascending) plus a parallel coeffs vector. This is the only thing the reduced-MPO sweeps read.

There is no fusion tree here: an ITOKey carries the running bond charge and vertex label, which for the caterpillar coupling are the tree (see irrepkey.jl).

OpSum.IndependentSVD — Type
IndependentSVD(trunc = nothing)

Bond-basis strategy: every bond is compressed independently, by an SVD of its coefficient matrix over the raw prefix/suffix classes of the term table.

trunc is a MatrixAlgebraKit.TruncationStrategy (e.g. truncrank, trunctol) or nothing (lossless). truncrank(k) means k retained indices per bond. Contrast SequentialSVD.

OpSum.InfiniteChain — Type
InfiniteChain(bases::AbstractVector)
InfiniteChain(B::Basis)

An infinite chain with a repeating unit cell of L = length(bases) sites, one operator Basis per site. Site i ∈ ℤ has basis bases[mod1(i, L)] (getindex wraps).

An operator compressed over one is a generating set: it represents Σ_{n ∈ ℤ} translate(H, n·L), so each translation class must appear exactly once (see unitcell_terms). Terms may reach past the cell: SS[1, 2] on a one-site cell names site 2.

LeftVertex{I}

A left (prefix) vertex of the persistent ITO graph: it entered the current site on incoming bond index link and applies the on-site ITO key key = (op, bond, vertex) here. There is no fermion/JW-string slot: the odd-parity charge on the virtual bond does that bookkeeping.

Obtained from project(h, B), from unplaced couple/dot, or from a SiteOperator. Place it with B[i] (sites i:i+K-1) or B[s₁, …, s_K].

OpSum.OperatorSum — Type
OperatorSum{I<:Sector, L<:AbstractLattice}

A Hamiltonian on a lattice: the lattice, a Terms bag of finite-range terms and an ExpSum of decaying channels. Build it with opsum(lat, terms...) or fill an OperatorSum(lat) with opsum!; mpo(H), islossless(H), instantiate(H) and jordan_mpo_tensors(H) take it. Terms are checked against the lattice as they enter. On an infinite chain H is a generating set, so terms must be charge-neutral and have K ≥ 1.

OpSum.PendingTerm — Type
PendingTerm{I<:Sector}

A product acting more than once on one site (a[i] * b[i], hop[i, i]), kept unevaluated until it enters an OperatorSum, where it is resolved in the sites' bases (products.jl). The letters are in written order with the running bond charges of the written-order caterpillar; the operator is coeff times that caterpillar's product.

OpSum.SVDBondAlgorithm — Type
SVDBondAlgorithm(trunc = nothing; sweep = IndependentSVD)

Algorithm selector: SVD-based bond subspace selection.

trunc is a MatrixAlgebraKit.TruncationStrategy (e.g. truncrank, trunctol) or nothing (lossless). Each bond's coefficient matrix is a charge-graded TensorMap, so svd_trunc truncates globally across sectors, respecting the quantum dimensions.

sweep picks the truncation semantics (it only matters when trunc !== nothing):

sweepmeaning of truncrank(k)
IndependentSVD (default)k retained indices per bond; every bond is compressed on the raw prefix/suffix classes, independently of its neighbours
SequentialSVDk retained indices after upstream truncation; each bond is compressed in the basis left over from the previous one, so an aggressive early truncation can starve downstream bonds

Both are exact when trunc === nothing. SVDBondAlgorithm(alg::BondStrategy) names the strategy directly.

source
OpSum.SequentialSVD — Type
SequentialSVD(trunc = nothing)

Bond-basis strategy: a sequential left-to-right sweep, where each bond is compressed in the basis left over from the bond before it.

trunc is a MatrixAlgebraKit.TruncationStrategy or nothing (lossless). Losslessly this agrees with IndependentSVD; under truncation the two differ by design: here truncrank(k) means "k indices after whatever upstream truncation already threw away", so an aggressive early truncation can starve downstream bonds. In exchange the sweep is incremental and reuses the persistent graph.

source
OpSum.SiteOperator — Type
SiteOperator{I<:Sector}

An operator on a single site, not yet placed: a sparse combination of ITO alphabet letters, Σₖ coeffs[k] · letters[k].

Obtained from project into a Basis, a builder such as spin_ops, or scalarop, and combined with + - * /. Place it on a site with A[i], which produces a Terms bag. The identity is the passthrough letter (unit(I), 1).

source
OpSum.SymbolicMPO — Type
SymbolicMPO{I<:Sector, L<:AbstractLattice}

A compressed MPO whose entries are symbolic, as mpo returns it: W[i] is the sparse bond matrix of site i, each stored entry a SiteOperator (a coefficient vector over the site's Basis), and W.bondsectors[i] the charge of each index of the bond to the right of site i. On a FiniteChain bond 0 is the trivial left boundary; on an InfiniteChain it is bond L.

W.start[i] and W.finish[i] are the indices of the two identity channels on bond i ("nothing placed yet" and "everything placed"), 0 where the compression spent no index on one; they are what the Jordan form needs. Both are empty when they are unknown (decaying channels on a finite chain).

instantiate(entry, basis) gives one entry's tensor; instantiate(W) gives MPSKit's FiniteMPOHamiltonian or InfiniteMPOHamiltonian once MPSKit is loaded.

OpSum.Term — Type
Term{I<:Sector}

A single term: sites (ascending, unique), one ITOKey each (letter, running bond charge and vertex label), and a reduced coeff. The caterpillar coupling tree is derived from the running bond charges (tree). Multiplicity-free fusion only.

Never mutated, so terms may share vectors. ==/hash ignore the coefficient, which is what makes summing coincident terms well defined.

source
OpSum.Terms — Type
Terms{I<:Sector}

The compressible ITO operator: a bag of Terms with no lattice. What A[i] and B[i] (placing a SiteOperator or a LocalOperator), couple and dot return, what +, -, * and / combine, and what opsum accumulates in one pass. The lattice enters when the terms are added to an OperatorSum.

Terms are appended as given; canonicalize! normalises in place. length, iteration, indexing and ≈ go through it, so they report the canonical operator (nterms_raw(ts) is the append count, for tests).

Base.:* — Method
a * b      # a, b :: Terms (placed operators)

The operator product of two placed operators, in the order written; it distributes over their terms. On different sites it is couple with the channel forced by the factors' charges, in any site order: under an abelian symmetry the braiding phase is inserted (for fermions, the anticommutation sign), under a non-abelian one the legs are re-coupled into site order, which can turn one term into several.

When the factors share a site, a[i] * b[i], the product is kept unevaluated and resolved in the site's Basis when the operator enters an OperatorSum. Until then the bag has no normal form, so length, iteration and ≈ throw; opsum(lat, a * b) is the way to look at it.

* throws when the channel is not forced: a product of two charged operators whose totals fuse to several sectors (non-abelian).

Base.getindex — Method
B[i]               -> Terms      # contiguous: sites i:i+K-1
B[s₁, s₂, …, s_K]  -> Terms      # explicit

Place a K-slot LocalOperator on the lattice: slot k acts on site sₖ, and the slots multiply in slot order. On strictly increasing sites placement relabels the sites and nothing else, so it is exact for any symmetry; sites inside a gap are pass-throughs, and pass-through slots are dropped.

Other placements are products in slot order (see *): out-of-order sites are reordered (braiding phase or re-coupling), and a repeated site, hop[i, i], is an on-site product kept unevaluated until the operator enters an OperatorSum.

isapprox(a::Terms, b::Terms; kwargs...)

LinearAlgebra.dot — Method
dot(a::Terms, b::Terms)
a · b

The Cartesian two-body scalar product of two single-site ITO operators: singlet coupling couple(a, b; to = unit(I)) times the Cartesian factor -√dim(c) (Sᵢ·Sⱼ = -√3 [S⊗S]⁽⁰⁾).

Either site order is accepted, for any symmetry: two legs coupling to the unit sector need no F-move to reorder. dot(a, b) == R · dot(b, a) with R = Rsymbol(cₐ, c_b, unit), which is +1 for bosonic integer charges (so spin chains are order-independent) and -1 for a pair of odd fermionic or half-integer SU(2) charges.

Unlike couple this does not distribute over composite operands: the factor -√dim(c) is per-letter.

OpSum._canonicalise_bond! — Method
_canonicalise_bond!(g, i, nout, site_dict, secW, nextedges_global, origins_global)

Reorder the assembled bond into canonical order: covered-left indices first, ordered by (incoming bond index, on-site ITOKey), then covered-right indices by their class name _rdesc. Permutes the block dictionary's columns, the bond charges, the forwarded edges and g.startidx/g.finishidx together.

Both keys are translation-invariant given that the incoming bond was canonically ordered (induction from the 1-dimensional boundary bond), so bond i and bond i+L of a periodic model get identically labelled bases, not merely isomorphic ones (see _canonicalise_rights!).

OpSum._canonicalise_rights! — Method
_canonicalise_rights!(g, i)

Renumber the live right vertices into canonical order (ascending _rdesc) and sort every adjacency list to match.

This makes the sweep a deterministic function of the canonical graph, not of its construction history. Hopcroft–Karp and König depend on the adjacency scan order, so without it isomorphic bonds can answer differently. Invisible on a finite chain (any minimum cover will do); fatal on a periodic lattice, where the bond basis converges only up to permutation and the extracted unit cell does not close. Components come out in first-left-vertex order with ascending ids, so canonical ids here plus canonical index order in _at_site! pin the whole sweep. Called before the sentinel is attached.

source
OpSum._force_finish! — Method
_force_finish!(cUbits, cVbits, localadj, pf) -> Bool

Move the finish class (component-local right index pf) into the cover, and drop every left vertex that this makes redundant. Returns whether anything changed.

A minimum cover may cover the finish class from the left instead (when it and one left vertex form an isolated matched pair, König visits neither), and that index would emit the last factor's letter, not the identity. Jordan form needs a channel emitting exactly 1 · id from the previous finish index, i.e. the covered-right reading. Forcing never costs: the cover grows by at most one, and it saves the padded index it would otherwise need. Dropping a left vertex whose neighbours are all covered is safe.

The start channel needs no counterpart: König never covers a degree-one right vertex, so the sentinel's left vertex L₀ is always covered-left (see §2.2 of research/persistent-graph-mpo.md), and it is never adjacent to the finish class, so forcing cannot remove it.

OpSum._irrep_channels — Method
_irrep_channels(tt::ITOTermTable{I}, N, strategy::IndependentSVD) -> (Ws, bondsectors, starts, finishes)

Per-bond-independent SVD reduced-MPO sweep: one coefficient matrix per bond over the raw prefix/suffix classes, keep the left singular vectors as that bond's compressed basis, then project the vertex operators into the compressed bases (W_op = U_{i-1}' · C_op · U_i per ITO letter).

Each bond's matrix is a charge-graded TensorMap C_b : Ppre ← Psuf, so svd_trunc does the per-sector SVD and the global truncation at once, and the retained space gives bondsectors. Entries stay ITO letters times scalars. trunc === nothing is lossless, and then agrees with SequentialSVD on internal bonds; under truncation they differ by design, see SVDBondAlgorithm.

Classes are named, not materialised: only the current bond's Θ(M) class assignment is held, plus one interned id → dense column dictionary per bond (Θ(N) for a finite-range model) so that phase 2 addresses the columns phase 1 built.

OpSum._irrep_graph_channels — Method
_irrep_graph_channels(tt::ITOTermTable{I}, N, strategy, jordan; channels) -> (Ws, bondsectors, starts, finishes)

The persistent-graph sweep with a bond-basis strategy (VertexCover or SequentialSVD). Ws::Vector{SparseMatrixCSC{SiteOperator{I}, Int}} and bondsectors::Vector{Vector{I}} are what mpo_terms / irrep_mpo_tensors consume; starts[i] / finishes[i] are the bond indices of the start ("nothing placed yet") and finish ("everything placed") channels at the bond right of site i, or 0 where the cover spent no index on them (always for SequentialSVD, whose bond basis mixes prefix states). channels are lowered exponentially decaying interactions (_lower_channels), which live beside the term table; a model may consist of nothing else.

jordan = true also forces the finish class into every cover (_force_finish!), which can change the bond basis away from the unconstrained minimum (rarely: a constant term next to two-site terms costs one index at the first bond, pinned in test/test_irrep_graph.jl).

OpSum._promote_pending! — Method
_promote_pending!(g, i)

Insert every not-yet-started term whose suffix class coincides with a live one at bond i.

_op_at_ito fills idle sites with a pass-through carrying the running bond charge, so a started term whose accumulated charge has fused back to unit(I) is indistinguishable, over its idle sites, from a term that has not started. If its remaining factors then coincide with a pending term's whole factor list, the two suffix classes are genuinely equal and the eager sweep merges them (covering the shared right vertex instead of spending a bond index). That merge has to be reproduced at the earliest bond where it applies; suffix-equality-from-i+1 is monotone in i, so probing every bond finds it exactly once. Cost: one hash probe per live right vertex (pendbysig is injective).

source
OpSum._rdesc — Method
_rdesc(g::ITOGraph, r, i) -> (kind, distance, shape, charge)

The canonical, translation-invariant name of right vertex r at bond i. An ordinary term class (kind = 0) is named by the distance from the bond to its first remaining factor (0 once exhausted), the shape of the remaining factor list (_rel_suffix_ids) and the running bond charge: two classes share a name exactly when they are translates of each other relative to their bonds. A geometric class (kind = 1) is named by its automaton state's interned name (ChannelState); the synthetic exhausted class shares kind = 0's exhausted name so it sorts and merges with exhausted term classes. The sentinel gets the reserved name (-1, -1, -1, unit(I)), which sorts first.

Translation-invariant twin of _suffix_ids: interns suffixes by shape, consing (gap to the next active site, ITOKey, tail id) instead of the absolute site. OpSum._suffix_ids — Method
_suffix_ids(tt::ITOTermTable{I}) -> Matrix{Int}

Intern every term's contiguous column suffixes: sufid[j, t] is a dense id for the factor list j:K of term t (0 for the exhausted suffix), built bottom-up in Θ(M·K).

Equal ids mean equal remaining factor lists, which is not equal suffix paths: _op_at_ito fills the idle sites before the first remaining factor with a pass-through carrying the running bond charge. A suffix path is therefore identified by (sufid[j₀, t], running bond charge).

OpSum.canonicalize! — Method
canonicalize!(ts::Terms) -> ts

Put ts in normal form in place: sort its terms (by sites, then keys, so coincident ones become adjacent), sum coincident ones, drop cancelled ones.

Assumes nothing about its input and is idempotent, but a repeat call re-sorts rather than returning early, hence the sweep normalises once, at the ITOTermTable boundary. Everything that observes the term set (length, iteration, ≈, ==, show) goes through this.

couple(a::Terms, b::Terms; to = unit(I))
couple(a::Terms, b::Terms, cs::Terms...; to = unit(I))   # channels must be forced

Left-nested (caterpillar) irrep coupling: extend the composite a (K ≥ 1 sites, with its running coupling charges) by one single-site operator b, fusing the running total of a with b's charge to to at a new vertex. to defaults to the unit sector (a Hamiltonian term); pass it explicitly to build a charged object. It is the primitive under * and placement. Its operands are charged single-site operators: a basis' charged elements (project(t, B) of a charged t : V ← V ⊗ Vect[I](c => 1)). Below, cd and c are a fermion's creation and annihilation operators and S the SU(2) spin vector.

The operands may be written in any site order: an out-of-order leg is inserted rather than appended, with the braiding phase that costs (for fermions the anticommutation sign), so couple(cd[i + 1], c[i]) == -couple(c[i], cd[i + 1]). Under a non-abelian symmetry the coupling is formed in written order and re-coupled into site order, so one pair of terms can give several, with F- and R-symbol coefficients.

Both operands may be composite (several terms, e.g. from project): the coupling distributes over every pair, and pairs whose charges cannot fuse to to are dropped. It is an error if no pair fuses. Each term of a must carry at least one charged operator, each of b be one charged site.

The variadic form takes the intermediate channels the charges force, and errors only where a channel is a genuine choice. Under an abelian symmetry (UniqueFusion) every one is forced; under a non-abelian one some still are:

couple(cd[1], c[2], cd[3], c[4])        # a charge-neutral four-fermion term
couple(S[1], S[2], S[3])                # j₁₂ = 1 is the only singlet channel

Where the channels are free the error lists them (couple_channels returns them as a value) and you nest, naming each one:

couple_channels(S[1], S[2], S[3], S[4])                                  # [(0,1), (1,1), (2,1)]
couple(couple(couple(S[1], S[2]; to = SU2Irrep(1)), S[3]; to = SU2Irrep(1)), S[4])

For fermions couple(cd[i], c[j]) is the physical c†ᵢcⱼ (Jordan–Wigner, sites 1..N). This is the bare fusion coupler: reduced coeff = va·vb, no normalization (the Cartesian convention lives in dot). GenericFusion and tree-structured (via) coupling are deferred.

OpSum.expterm — Method
expterm(t::Terms; decay, exitsite = maximum(sites), string = nothing) -> ExpSum

An exponentially decaying interaction generated from the representative term(s) t by stretching the gap just before exitsite geometrically, with ratio decay per site:

SS = spin_ops(SU2Irrep).S_exchange
expterm(SS[1, 2]; decay = 0.5)                 # Σ_{i<j} 0.5^{j-i-1} S⃗_i·S⃗_j
expterm(SS[1, 3]; decay = 0.5)                 # the same, but never closer than next-nearest neighbours

t must be fully coupled with at least two active sites (it carries the fusion tree); a composite operand yields one channel per term. exitsite must be an active site of the representative and not its first. string is a charge-neutral SiteOperator carried by every site of the stretched gap (default: the identity). decay must satisfy 0 < |λ| < 1. The lattice supplies the translation period: L on an InfiniteChain, one site on a finite chain.

OpSum.jordan_mpo_tensors — Method
jordan_mpo_tensors(H::OperatorSum[, alg]) -> Vector{<:SparseBlockTensorMap}

jordan_mpo_tensors(mpo(H, alg)): compress H (see mpo) and emit it in Jordan form, one BlockTensorKit.SparseBlockTensorMap per site (of the chain, or of the unit cell), W_i : B_{i-1} ⊗ V_i ← V_i ⊗ B_i, whose virtual legs are SumSpaces with one level Vect[I](charge => 1) per bond index, ordered (start channel, everything else, finish channel) with the identity at (1, 1) and (end, end). On a finite chain the boundary bonds are one-dimensional and trivially charged, so H must have total charge unit(I).

Diagonal unit-coefficient pass-throughs are emitted as TensorKit.BraidingTensors, so a consumer can store them as scalars. This is the shape MPSKit's JordanMPOTensor consumes (instantiate(mpo(H))); use irrep_mpo_tensors for dense per-site TensorMaps.

An SVDBondAlgorithm bond basis mixes prefix states, so neither identity channel is a basis vector and both are padded at every bond; a truncation that empties a bond is rejected, since the identity corners need an index. Exponentially decaying channels on a finite chain have no Jordan form yet.

OpSum.mpo — Method
mpo(H::OperatorSum[, alg]) -> SymbolicMPO

Compress the Hamiltonian H — an OperatorSum, which carries its lattice — into a SymbolicMPO, on a FiniteChain or an InfiniteChain, with or without exponentially decaying channels (see expterm):

W = mpo(opsum(FiniteChain(B, N), h))
instantiate(W[2][1, 3], B)          # one entry's tensor
using MPSKit; instantiate(W)        # an MPSKit.FiniteMPOHamiltonian

On a FiniteChain the operator is H as written, on N = length(lat) sites. On an InfiniteChain of L sites H is a generating set: the operator represented is Σ_{n ∈ ℤ} translate(H, n·L), so each translation class must appear exactly once (see unitcell_terms); that is checked here, as it can only be seen across the whole set. The letters were already checked against the sites' bases when the terms entered H.

A channel's period is fixed by the lattice: on an InfiniteChain of L sites its entry and exit step by L, while on a FiniteChain every site is a possible entry, i.e. the operator represented is the whole geometric sum truncated to the chain — chain_terms(H) spells it out. Each channel becomes a single bond index with λ on its diagonal, so its cost is independent of the interaction range.

The two identity channels of every bond are identified, as the Jordan form (jordan_mpo_tensors, instantiate(W)) needs; on a finite chain the finish channel is forced into the cover for that. With decaying channels on a finite chain they are not, so that SymbolicMPO has no Jordan form.

alg is BipartiteAlgorithm() (the default, lossless minimum vertex cover) or SVDBondAlgorithm(trunc; sweep) (see BondStrategy), available on a FiniteChain without channels only.

OpSum.mpo_terms — Method
mpo_terms(Ws, bondsectors; leftidx = 1, rightidx = nothing) -> Terms

Reconstruct the operator a reduced MPO generates (inverse of mpo at the reduced level): enumerate every path through the bond matrices, multiply the coefficients along it, and read the active ITO letters with their outgoing bond charges (the caterpillar running bonds). OpSum.mpo_terms_window — Method
mpo_terms_window(H::SymbolicMPO, ncells::Int) -> Terms

The terms an infinite MPO generates inside a window of ncells cells: tile the cell and enumerate the paths from the start to the finish channel. This pins coefficients and fusion trees, and is a sandwich against window_terms, not an equality:

  • every term produced is a translate inside the window at its exact coefficient;
  • every translate ending at least R + 1 sites before the right edge is produced.

The gap is a property of the compression: when a term's suffix class is shared with a longer term, its coefficient is folded onto the shared channel's trailing pass-through (the pending/started collision, research/persistent-graph-mpo.md §2.2), so a path can run up to R sites past the term's support.
