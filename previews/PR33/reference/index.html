OpSum.AbstractLattice — Type
AbstractLattice

Supertype of FiniteChain and InfiniteChain, the lattices an OperatorSum can carry. A lattice names the operator Basis of every site.

source
OpSum.Basis — Type
Basis(ops::AbstractVector; atol = 0, rtol = 1e-12, complete = false)

An ordered basis of on-site operators on one space V: the alphabet that project expresses operators in. Each entry of ops is a charge-neutral operator V ← V (or a plain matrix, without symmetry), used as an element, or V ⊗ V ← V ⊗ V, factorised into its charged single-site pieces (for S⁺ ⊗ S⁻ the pieces S⁺ and S⁻).

Element 1 is the identity; single-site entries follow in the order given, then the factorised pieces. An entry parallel to an earlier element of its charge is dropped; the elements of one charge must be linearly independent (checked) but need not be orthogonal. project throws for an operator outside the span. With complete = true the basis is extended by matrix units to span every operator on V.

OpSum.FiniteChain — Type

FiniteChain(B::Basis, N::Integer)

An open chain with one operator Basis per site (all of one symmetry); the letters of a term on site i index into bases[i]. getindex does not wrap. FiniteChain(B, N) is the uniform chain; a bare vector of bases is accepted anywhere a FiniteChain is.

OpSum.LocalOperator — Type
LocalOperator{I<:Sector}

An operator on K consecutive slots, not yet placed on the lattice: a Terms bag on the sites 1:K, every term occupying all of them (a slot it does not act on holds the pass-through letter). Obtained from project(h, B), from unplaced couple/dot, or from a SiteOperator. Place it with B[i] (sites i:i+K-1) or B[s₁, …, s_K].

OpSum.OperatorSum — Type
OperatorSum{I<:Sector, L<:AbstractLattice}

A Hamiltonian on a lattice: a Terms bag of finite-range terms plus an ExpSum of decaying channels. Build it with opsum(lat, terms...) or fill an OperatorSum(lat) with opsum!. On an infinite chain terms must be charge-neutral with K ≥ 1.

Algorithm selector for SVD bond truncation (trunc as in IndependentSVD; svd_trunc truncates globally across charge sectors). sweep is IndependentSVD or SequentialSVD, which differ in the meaning of truncrank(k); SVDBondAlgorithm(strategy) names a strategy directly.

OpSum.SequentialSVD — Type
SequentialSVD(trunc = nothing)

Left-to-right sweep compressing each bond in the basis left by the previous one, so truncrank(k) keeps k indices after upstream truncation. Agrees with IndependentSVD when lossless.

OpSum.SiteOperator — Type
SiteOperator{I<:Sector}

An unplaced single-site operator Σₖ coeffs[k] · letters[k], from project, spin_ops and friends or scalarop; combine with + - * / and place with A[i] (a Terms bag).

source
OpSum.SymbolicMPO — Type
SymbolicMPO{I<:Sector, L<:AbstractLattice}

A compressed MPO with symbolic entries, as mpo returns it: W[i] is the sparse bond matrix of site i (entries are SiteOperators) and W.bondsectors[i] the charge of each index of the bond to its right (on an InfiniteChain bond L is the one left of site 1). W.start[i] and W.finish[i] are the two identity-channel indices on bond i (0 if the compression spent none; both vectors are empty when unknown, i.e. decaying channels on a finite chain).

instantiate(W) gives MPSKit's FiniteMPOHamiltonian or InfiniteMPOHamiltonian once MPSKit is loaded.

OpSum.Term — Type
Term{I<:Sector}

One term: sites (ascending, unique), one ITOKey each (letter, running bond charge, vertex label) and a reduced coeff. Multiplicity-free fusion only. Never mutated, so terms share vectors; ==/hash ignore the coefficient, which is what makes summing coincident terms well defined.

OpSum.Terms — Type
Terms{I<:Sector}

The compressible ITO operator: a lattice-free bag of Terms, returned by placement (A[i]), couple and dot, and combined by + - * /. Terms are appended as given; canonicalize! normalises in place, and length, iteration, indexing and ≈ go through it.

OpSum.VertexCover — Type
VertexCover()

Base.:* — Method
a * b      # a, b :: Terms (placed operators)

The operator product of two placed operators, in the order written, distributing over their terms. On different sites it is couple with the channel forced by the factors' charges, in any site order (braiding phase for abelian symmetries, re-coupling into site order otherwise, which can give several terms). A shared site, a[i] * b[i], stays unevaluated until the operator enters an OperatorSum; until then the bag has no normal form, so length, iteration and ≈ throw.

* throws when the channel is not forced (two charged totals fusing to several sectors).

Base.getindex — Method
B[i]               -> Terms      # contiguous: sites i:i+K-1
B[s₁, s₂, …, s_K]  -> Terms      # explicit

Place a K-slot LocalOperator on the lattice: slot k acts on site sₖ. On strictly increasing sites this only relabels, so it is exact for any symmetry (gap sites are pass-throughs). Other placements are products in slot order (see *): out-of-order sites are reordered, and a repeated site, hop[i, i], is an on-site product kept unevaluated until it enters an OperatorSum.

source
LinearAlgebra.dot — Method
dot(a::Terms, b::Terms)
a · b

The Cartesian scalar product of two single-site operators: the singlet couple(a, b) times -√dim(c) (Sᵢ·Sⱼ = -√3 [S⊗S]⁽⁰⁾). Either site order is accepted; swapping costs the R-symbol Rsymbol(cₐ, c_b, unit) (-1 for odd fermionic or half-integer SU(2) charges). Unlike couple, it does not distribute over composite operands.

OpSum.couple — Method
couple(a::Terms, b::Terms; to = unit(I))
couple(a::Terms, b::Terms, cs::Terms...; to = unit(I))

Left-nested (caterpillar) coupling of the composite a with the charged single-site operator b, fusing their charges to to (default: the unit sector, a Hamiltonian term). The operands may be written in any site order: an out-of-order leg picks up its braiding phase, so couple(cd[i + 1], c[i]) == -couple(c[i], cd[i + 1]). Both operands may be composite; pairs that cannot fuse to to are dropped, and it is an error if none do.

The variadic form takes every intermediate channel the charges force and errors where one is a genuine choice (see couple_channels); nest couple to name it. For SU(2), couple(S[1], S[2], S[3]) is forced, couple(S[1], S[2], S[3], S[4]) is not.

source
OpSum.couple — Method
couple(a, b, rest...; to = unit(I))   # a, b, rest… : SiteOperator or LocalOperator

The unplaced coupling: the placed caterpillar on consecutive slots, returned as a LocalOperator, so couple(cd, c)[i, j] ≈ couple(cd[i], c[j]). A pass-through (scalar) part of an operand occupies its slot. Every operand after the first must be single-slot; to and the variadic forced-channel form are those of the placed couple.

OpSum.expterm — Method
expterm(t::Terms; decay, exitsite = maximum(sites), string = nothing) -> ExpSum

An exponentially decaying interaction generated from the representative term(s) t, stretching the gap just before exitsite geometrically with ratio decay (0 < |decay| < 1). t must be fully coupled on at least two sites; exitsite is one of its active sites, not the first. string is a charge-neutral SiteOperator on every site of the stretched gap (default identity). The translation period is L on an InfiniteChain, one site on a finite chain.

OpSum.irrep_mpo_tensors — Method
irrep_mpo_tensors(W::SymbolicMPO) -> Vector{<:AbstractTensorMap}

Dense symmetric site tensors W_i : B_{i-1} ⊗ V_i ← V_i ⊗ B_i (MPSKit convention) of an mpo, each entry read in its site's basis; the boundary bonds of a finite chain are one-dimensional. OpSum.jordan_mpo_tensors — Method
jordan_mpo_tensors(H::OperatorSum[, alg]) -> Vector{<:SparseBlockTensorMap}

jordan_mpo_tensors(mpo(H, alg)): the compressed MPO in Jordan form, one SparseBlockTensorMap per site (of the chain or the unit cell), W_i : B_{i-1} ⊗ V_i ← V_i ⊗ B_i, with bond indices ordered (start channel, everything else, finish channel) and identities at (1, 1) and (end, end) emitted as BraidingTensors. On a finite chain H must have total charge unit(I). This is the shape MPSKit's JordanMPOTensor consumes; use irrep_mpo_tensors for dense TensorMaps. Not available for decaying channels on a finite chain; a truncating SVDBondAlgorithm that empties a bond is rejected.

source
OpSum.mpo — Method
mpo(H::OperatorSum[, alg]) -> SymbolicMPO

Compress H into a SymbolicMPO, on either lattice, with or without exponentially decaying channels (expterm).

On a FiniteChain the operator is H as written. On an InfiniteChain of L sites H is a generating set: it represents Σ_{n ∈ ℤ} translate(H, n·L), so each translation class must appear once (see unitcell_terms). A decaying channel becomes one bond index with λ on its diagonal; on a FiniteChain it represents the geometric sum truncated to the chain (chain_terms).

The two identity channels of every bond are identified (the Jordan form needs them); on a finite chain the finish channel is forced into the cover for that, except with decaying channels.

alg is BipartiteAlgorithm() (default, lossless minimum vertex cover) or SVDBondAlgorithm(trunc; sweep) (finite chains without channels only).

source
OpSum.mpo_tensormap — Method
mpo_tensormap(Ts::AbstractVector) -> AbstractTensorMap

Contract MPO site tensors (irrep_mpo_tensors, or jordan_mpo_tensors after map(TensorMap, ·)) into the N-site operator in instantiate's leg convention, so that mpo_tensormap(irrep_mpo_tensors(mpo(H))) ≈ instantiate(H). Exponential in N.

OpSum.spin_basis — Function
spin_basis(symmetry = Trivial; spin = 1//2) -> Basis

The operator basis of TensorKitTensors' spin_space(symmetry; spin): the identity, then S⁺, S⁻ and Sᶻ (no symmetry, U(1)), or S⃗ (SU(2)), or the spin-flip-graded pieces (ℤ₂), completed to span every operator on the space (the higher multipoles for spin > 1/2). Memoised.

See spin_ops for the operators expressed in it.

OpSum.spin_ops — Function
spin_ops(symmetry = Trivial; spin = 1//2) -> NamedTuple
fermion_ops(symmetry = Trivial) -> NamedTuple
boson_ops(symmetry = Trivial; cutoff) -> NamedTuple
hubbard_ops(particle_symmetry = Trivial, spin_symmetry = Trivial) -> NamedTuple
tj_ops(particle_symmetry = Trivial, spin_symmetry = Trivial; slave_fermion = false) -> NamedTuple

Every charge-neutral operator TensorKitTensors defines on that site for that symmetry, projected into the matching stock basis (spin_basis, …) and named after its TensorKitTensors function: a single-site operator is a SiteOperator (ops.S_z[i]), a two-site one an unplaced LocalOperator (ops.S_exchange[i, j]). Operators the symmetry does not admit as neutral ones (S_z under SU(2), S_plus under U(1)) are absent. Memoised.
